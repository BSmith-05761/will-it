# U.S. Will Drafting — Legal Requirements and Operator Notes

Purpose: Practical, jurisdiction‑aware checklist for drafting and executing wills in the United States, plus key compliance notes for operating a will‑prep product. This is general guidance, not legal advice. Laws vary by state and change over time; always verify the current statute/rules before use.

## Core Validity (Most States)
- Capacity: Testator is typically 18+ and of sound mind (understands property, natural heirs, and the disposition being made).
- Writing: A written will. Typewritten is universally acceptable. Handwritten (holographic) wills are valid only in some states and follow special rules.
- Signature: Signed by the testator. Some states allow a proxy to sign at the testator’s direction and in the testator’s presence.
- Witnesses: Commonly two competent witnesses who witness the testator sign (or acknowledge the signature) and then sign. Many states require disinterested witnesses (not beneficiaries) or reduce/void gifts to interested witnesses.
- Notary: Usually not required for validity, but a notarized self‑proving affidavit (where permitted) allows admission to probate without live witness testimony.

Commonly included clauses
- Revocation: Clear statement revoking prior wills/codicils.
- Executor: Appointment of personal representative (and alternates) with independent administration powers where permitted.
- Guardianship: Nomination of guardian of the person/estate for minors or dependents (and alternates).
- Bequests: Specific gifts (optional) and a residuary clause disposing of all remaining property; set representation method (per stirpes/per capita) and survivorship period if desired.
- Tangible personal property memorandum: Enable a separate list for small items (only if allowed by state statute; keep references consistent).
- Digital assets: Authorization to access/manage digital accounts (RUFADAA‑style authority in adopting states).
- No‑contest: Optional and enforceability varies by state.

Limitations that override the will
- Spousal protections: Elective share/community‑property rules may prevent full spousal disinheritance.
- Non‑probate transfers: Assets passing by beneficiary designation (retirement, life insurance), joint tenancy, TOD/POD accounts, and trusts do not follow the will—coordinate designations.

Special types
- Holographic wills: Accepted in some states; often require material portions in handwriting and signature. Risky for users; steer to typewritten wills.
- Nuncupative (oral) wills: Rare, limited circumstances (e.g., imminent peril); not a general path.

## Execution Ceremony (Paper)
Checklist (adapt for state rules):
- Confirm capacity, identity, and voluntariness.
- Print final document with page numbers; include an attestation clause.
- Gather two competent, disinterested witnesses (more if required). All parties in the same room unless state permits alternatives.
- Testator signs in presence of witnesses (or acknowledges prior signature). Witnesses sign in presence of testator (some states require all in each other’s presence).
- Complete a self‑proving affidavit if permitted (testator + witnesses sign before a notary). Attach to the will.
- Store original securely; record location and executor contact.

## Electronic Wills and Remote Notarization (Varies by State)
- ESIGN/UETA do not automatically make e‑wills valid; states need specific e‑will statutes (e.g., UEWA or bespoke).
- E‑will statutes specify permitted execution methods: remote witnessing/notarization, audiovisual presence standards, storage/custodian duties, and self‑proving requirements.
- Remote Online Notarization (RON) is widely permitted for general notarizations; e‑will use depends on state statute.
- Florida‑style regimes may require a qualified custodian and strict storage/audit controls; don’t offer e‑wills in such states unless those duties are met.

Platform guidance
- Geofence execution flows by state capability (paper only vs. e‑will allowed; remote witnessing allowed or not; RON vendor requirements).
- Keep an evidence package: video session, identity proofing results, time stamps, audit logs, and storage metadata where required.

## Jurisdiction, Property, and Choice‑of‑Law
- Domicile controls many personal will requirements; real property often follows the law of its situs (state where located). Ancillary probate may be required for out‑of‑state real property.
- Community property: Treat spousal property interests carefully (commonly: AZ, CA, ID, LA, NV, NM, TX, WA, WI; AK opt‑in). Confirm current law.
- 120‑hour survivorship/Simultaneous death rules: Common to prevent double probate; set explicitly if desired.

## Guardians, Fiduciaries, and Trusts
- Executor/personal representative: Choose primary and alternates; consider bond waiver; allow independent administration where permitted.
- Trustees (if any testamentary trusts): Define powers, distributions (HEMS standard), and successors; consider special needs trusts for disabled beneficiaries; consider UTMA/UGMA alternatives for small gifts to minors.
- Digital assets agent: RUFADAA authorization for access/disclosure (may be executor or separate agent).

## Taxes, Debts, and Apportionment
- Define debt payment preferences and order of abatement.
- Set tax apportionment (paid from residuary vs equitable apportionment among recipients).
- Consider marital deduction/QTIP directions (advanced planning topic; often requires counsel).

## Revocation and Updates
- Later will or codicil controls; physical destruction with intent can revoke.
- Revisit on life events (marriage, divorce, birth/adoption, move, major asset changes).

## Storage and Aftercare
- Keep signed original safe and findable; inform executor.
- Consider filing/registration only if appropriate in your state. Provide a memorandum template and instructions for updating beneficiary designations.

## Operator Compliance (Starting a U.S. Wills Product)
Business models
- Pure self‑help software: Users fill state‑specific forms; you do not select forms or give individualized advice. Prominent “not a law firm/no legal advice” disclosures. Some states regulate non‑lawyer document preparers (e.g., AZ, NV, CA) with registration/bonding and mandated disclosures.
- Hybrid software + independent attorney review: Users can buy attorney review/edit/signing guidance; avoid fee‑splitting; follow advertising/referral ethics. Platform/SaaS or flat listing fees are typical.
- Lawyer‑owned or ABS (where allowed): Operate as a law firm or obtain alternative business structure authorization (e.g., AZ ABS, UT sandbox); still require licensed lawyers in each practice state.

Red‑lines (UPL guardrails)
- Do not select forms or tailor legal language for a specific user unless a licensed lawyer is engaged for that user’s state.
- Provide neutral explanations and education; route complex fact patterns (special needs, disinheritance, taxable estates, multi‑state real estate) to attorneys.

E‑sign and consumer consent
- ESIGN‑compliant consent and retention for electronic delivery; present clear, conspicuous consent and provide copies.
- If using subscriptions, comply with state Automatic Renewal Laws and the FTC’s “click‑to‑cancel” requirements when effective.

Privacy and security
- Handle sensitive personal data. Build to a recognized security baseline (e.g., SOC 2/NIST‑aligned controls) and track multi‑state privacy and breach‑notification rules.

RON and e‑will vendors
- Integrate with approved RON providers where permitted; follow state ID proofing, audio‑video recording, journal retention, and storage rules.
- For e‑wills with custodial obligations (e.g., Florida), partner with or become a compliant qualified custodian before offering the feature.

## Intake Checklist (What to Collect Before Drafting)
- Identity: full legal name, aliases, DOB, domicile address, contact.
- Family: marital/partner status; prior marriages; children/dependents (names, DOBs, special needs); guardians (primary + alternates).
- Fiduciaries: executor(s), trustee(s), digital assets agent, funeral agent.
- Assets: real property, bank/brokerage, retirement/insurance, business interests, vehicles, tangible/digital assets; ownership and situs.
- Designations: current beneficiaries for non‑probate assets; identify conflicts.
- Gifts: specific gifts, charitable gifts, residuary plan, contingents, survivorship period.
- Trusts: minors’ trust/UTMA, special needs, pet trust, education trust; trustees and terms.
- Taxes/debts: apportionment, abatement preferences.
- Execution: witness count/disinterest, notary/self‑proving options, paper vs electronic.
